# AI-assistance and data-provenance declaration

I, Niu Tong, initiated and directed this project. I defined the problem, wrote initial examples of everyday music requests, and specified the intent fields and song-label criteria I wanted to test. I decided the product scope, the conditions the catalog could and could not support, and the evaluation approach. I reviewed disputed interpretations, approved the V2 answers before the formal run, listened to and checked all 35 tracks, assessed the recommended playlists, and interpreted the results. I kept the frozen answers unchanged after seeing the scores.

I used AI tools to assist with revision and implementation. I provided my examples, criteria, and requirements; checked the responses against my intentions; questioned interpretations I disagreed with; and decided what to revise or retain. The final project decisions and judgments are mine.

- **Request examples and intent annotations:** On 2026-10-01, I used **Claude Sonnet 5.5** to help revise and extend the request examples and annotation ideas I had supplied. I checked the resulting 42 cases, resolved ambiguous meanings, and approved the final V2 annotations. The cases are examples, not quotations from recruited users; there were zero participants, and the persona IDs are fictional.
- **Song labels:** On 2026-10-01, I used **Kimi K3** for candidate labels using the listening criteria and examples I provided. I treated Kimi’s suggestions as drafts and personally listened to all 35 tracks before checking and approving the labels. I also checked the melody-change time points. `verified` means my single-author review, not agreement among independent listeners or objective ground truth.
- **Code and documentation:** **Codex** assisted with writing and revising code, tests, debugging tools, and English text based on my requirements and feedback. I reviewed the outputs, directed corrections, selected the version to evaluate, and remain responsible for the submitted repository and conclusions.

For the English submission, I directed Codex to translate the repository text; an OpenRouter translation model assisted with that task. The current English files are reading translations, not a new evaluation. **Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite via OpenRouter** was the runtime model in the recorded experiment, separate from the tools used during preparation. The exact Chinese inputs, operational prompt, and frozen results remain available in [commit `a39e3fc`](https://github.com/Tong16-lab/music-intent-playlist/tree/a39e3fc). The repository links to songs but does not redistribute audio, and it does not claim evidence of improvement in real listeners' musical taste.
